# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audience is hiring managers, product leaders, AI product teams, and collaborators evaluating Abhyudaya Singh's product judgment. They are reviewing quickly, often in one sitting, and need to see credible proof that the work is shipped, inspectable, and grounded in real product trade-offs.

Secondary users include peers, recruiters, and future collaborators who want to understand the portfolio thesis, browse shipped prototypes, and follow build notes or PM teardown work.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio exists to position Abhyudaya Singh as a fintech operator who ships production systems and now builds with AI. The site should show working prototypes, case studies, evals, architecture decisions, and honest boundaries.

Success means a visitor can quickly understand:

- what Abhyudaya builds
- why the work is product-relevant
- where AI is intentionally constrained
- what evidence supports each project
- how to inspect the repo, prototype, case study, resume, and contact links

## Brand Personality

Bold, precise, builder-minded.

The portfolio should feel like a serious product lab run by a PM who works close to implementation. It should be sharper than a generic resume site, more concrete than a personal blog, and more professional than a playful demo gallery.

The voice should be direct and specific. It should avoid hype, vague AI claims, and over-polished corporate language. The strongest impression should be: this person can frame ambiguous product problems, ship working systems, and explain why the system behaves the way it does.

## Anti-references

Avoid:

- generic SaaS landing-page templates
- AI-gradient wallpaper and neural-network decoration
- overuse of cards, chips, and repeated tiny uppercase section labels
- beige editorial sameness without a distinct product-builder point of view
- fake dashboards, fake metrics, or fake deployment claims
- portfolio pages that only say "strategy" without showing shipped artifacts
- product UI that feels decorative instead of task-focused
- case studies that read like project logs instead of product judgment

## Design Principles

1. Show proof before polish.
   The design should make shipped work, evals, guardrails, and artifacts easy to inspect.

2. Make constraints visible.
   AI boundaries, fallback behavior, and mocked vs real functionality should be clear without defensive footnotes.

3. Use bold hierarchy, not loud decoration.
   The portfolio should feel memorable through structure, contrast, and strong product language rather than generic visual effects.

4. Separate brand surfaces from product surfaces.
   The portfolio homepage and case studies can be expressive. The CraveWise app should remain functional, clear, and interaction-led.

5. Professional does not mean generic.
   The site should feel credible to hiring managers while still carrying a distinct product-builder identity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for text contrast, keyboard focus, and responsive layout. Motion should be minimal, purposeful, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences. Links and CTAs should have standalone meaning. Disabled or unavailable items should be visually distinct from active links.
